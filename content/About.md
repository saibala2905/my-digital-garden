## How I Approach Problems

I believe good engineering starts with **understanding the problem before choosing the technology**.

When I encounter a problem in an unfamiliar domain, I deliberately step outside my existing comfort zone. I spend time understanding the domain, talking to stakeholders, asking questions, challenging assumptions, and identifying the actual problem behind the initial requirement.

Once I have a reasonable understanding of the problem, I move into **research, experimentation, and prototyping**.

I typically explore:

- Existing solutions and industry practices
- Relevant research and technical literature
- Emerging technologies and frameworks
- Architectural possibilities
- Automation opportunities
- Constraints and failure modes
- Potential AI and agentic approaches

I prefer building **small, focused proofs of concept early** rather than spending too much time designing a large system based purely on assumptions.

These experiments help me validate ideas, understand limitations, and gradually evolve an approach into a more complete system.

## From Prototype to System

I enjoy the complete journey from an early idea to a working system.

A typical development cycle for me looks something like:

```text
Problem
   ↓
Domain Understanding
   ↓
Research & Exploration
   ↓
Architecture
   ↓
Rapid POC
   ↓
Validation
   ↓
Iteration
   ↓
Engineering
   ↓
Testing & Evaluation
   ↓
Deployment
   ↓
Continuous Improvement
```

Throughout this process, I value **continuous feedback**.

Frequent demonstrations and conversations with stakeholders help validate whether the system is solving the right problem, while technical experimentation helps determine whether the chosen approach is actually viable.

I find this combination of **technical exploration + stakeholder feedback + iterative engineering** particularly effective when working on emerging AI use cases where requirements and possibilities are still evolving.
